## A1. Consumer-Rules Desk — "the new rule that changes what you're owed"

1. Concept & angle: One beat — the rule pipeline that moves money between companies and
   consumers (DOT refunds, EU261 reform, FTC junk-fee/click-to-cancel, Montreal Convention
   limits, CAA rules). Angle: not "news happened" but "what this rule entitles YOU to, with
   the citation on screen." Every regulatory action is a video; every rule change obsoletes
   old advice (built-in refresh demand).
2. Audience/geos: US (DOT/FTC/CBP), UK+EU (UK261/EU261, CAA), Canada (APPR), Australia
   (airline ombudsman schemes). Mass consumer — anyone who flies, books hotels, or holds
   subscriptions. effectively all adults.
3. Audience size/demand: DOT maintains a standing "Airline Family Seating Dashboard" [14] —
   a permanent government consumer resource; Martin Lewis's 11-year-old compensation video
   still draws 103K views [57]; "12 Airport Rule Changes in 2026 That Nobody's Talking About"
   39.4K views in 5 months [58] on a channel of that name. Air-help/claim companies (AirHelp,
   AirFairness [59]) exist solely because the audience searches this constantly.
4. Recurring developments (dated): Council/Parliament landmark agreement on stronger EU air
   passenger rights, Jun 15 2026 [11]; DOT refunds final rule Federal Register Dec 5 2025 with
   ongoing 2026 enforcement posture [12][13]; FTC junk-fee rule (bipartisan, Dec 2024; live
   pricing enforcement through 2025–26) [15][16]; click-to-cancel taking effect July 2026 [17].
   Recurring calendar: quarterly DOT enforcement reports, monthly state AG actions, continuous
   EU trilogue steps. Estimated sustainable pipeline: 3–6 substantial videos/week (order-of-
   magnitude, from one day's dated sampling — see METHOD-P2 limitation).
5. Sample titles (real topics / repeatable formats):
   - "The EU just rewrote flight compensation (June 2026 agreement, article-by-article) [11]"
   - "DOT's refund rule is live: the 4 triggers that force automatic cash back [12][13]"
   - "Click-to-cancel is in effect: what subscriptions must do now, and the one loophole [17]"
   - "Junk fees 101: which fees are now illegal at hotels and checkouts [15][16]"
   - "Family seating: which airlines the DOT dashboard says guarantee it free [14]"
   - "EU261 vs UK261 vs APPR: the same delay, three payouts — worked examples"
   - "The Montreal Convention baggage cap, and why 'limited release' tags matter"
   - Format: "Rule Desk" — every episode one rule: what changed, who it covers, what to file.
6. Competitors & recent performance: Martin Lewis (broad money, 103K on an 11-year-old video
   [57]); Airport NOW (39.4K/5mo on rules roundup [58]); AirHelp's own channel (649 views/3mo
   guide — vendor, low reach [59]); news clips (955–1.4K per story on bag fees, raw/
   yt-competition-p2.json). No dedicated faceless "rule desk" channel found in 2 queries of
   structured SERP data; the closest dedicated attempt (airfairness) sits at 103 views [59].
7. Content gap: Rules are covered as fragments by news channels (single-story clips, low
   views) and by vendors with conflicts of interest (claim companies want commissions). A
   citation-first neutral explainer that consolidates "what you are owed under current law"
   — updated as rules change — has no dedicated owner in the sampled SERPs.
8. Advertiser potential: FACT: travel cards and booking platforms monetize travel-intent
   audiences at $100–800/approved CPA [9]; claim companies sponsor rights content (AirHelp
   exists in-SERP [59]). RPM: no measured number for this niche; finance-band context [6]
   ASSUMPTION at best. CPM vs RPM distinction maintained.
9. Weekly pipeline & production: 3–6/week feasible: rule-docket monitoring (Federal Register
   RSS, DOT/FTC/CAA press pages, EU Council calendar), one producer + narration over animated
   decision trees ("were you owed? flowchart"), document-callout graphics, jurisdiction
   comparison tables. Fully faceless; high citation density = differentiator.
10. AIO queries to test later (untested): kit P1, e.g. "how much is EU261 compensation for a
    3 hour delay in 2026", "what does the DOT automatic refund rule require", "are airlines
    required to seat families together for free in 2026" — full 10 in AIOV-GAP-KIT-P2.md.
    Rationale for suspicion: answers are amount- and threshold-specific and just changed [11][12].

   (parallel hard markets). Massive adult audience with direct wallet impact.
3. Audience size/demand: Insurify projects increases in 32 states for 2026; average full
   coverage now $2,237/yr, with 7 states + DC above $3,000 [21]; State Farm's Illinois
   homeowners +8% covered Oct 5 2026 [22]; one state's rates "jumped nearly 43%" headline
   [23]; insurance leads cost $24–40/call — proof of sustained buying intent [55].
4. Recurring developments (dated): State Farm IL +8% (Oct 5 2026) [22]; CA DOI one-year
   non-renewal moratorium page (standing policy machinery) [24]; Insurify 32-state H1-2026
   report [21]. Every rate filing (dozens/week nationally), every regulator press release,
   every catastrophe season renews the beat. Est. 4–8 videos/week of substance.
5. Sample titles:
   - "State Farm just raised Illinois 8% — here's the filing behind it [22]"
   - "The 43% state: which premiums exploded and the perils named in the filing [23]"
   - "California's non-renewal moratorium, explained clause by clause [24]"
   - "32 states are projected to rise in 2026: the map, and the per-state math [21]"

   - "Wildfire smoke vs fire: the coverage distinction that decides claims"
   - Format: "Rate Watch" — monthly state-by-state approved-changes roundup.
6. Competitors & recent performance: Insurify/quotelyleads-class vendors publish reports but
   are lead-gen, not channels; news clips per rate event (FOX 32 205 views on bag fees-
   adjacent; insurance clips similar, raw/yt-competition-p2.json). YouTube search for
   "homeowners insurance increase 2026" returns news fragments, not a dedicated channel —

7. Content gap: Nobody consolidates filings into monthly explainers; vendors have lead-gen
   conflicts; regulators publish PDFs nobody reads. A neutral "what changed in YOUR state"
   desk is unowned.
8. Advertiser potential: FACT: insurance is a $24–40/call lead market [55]; comparison
   platforms and agents sponsor heavily. ASSUMPTION: highest natural ad fit of the 10;
   RPM unmeasured; YMYL-adjacent care needed.
9. Weekly pipeline & production: 4–8/week: rate-filing monitors (state DOI press feeds),
