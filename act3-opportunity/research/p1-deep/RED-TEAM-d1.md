# Red-Team — Direction 1 differentiation claim (manual-grounded interactive decision-tree EXECUTION)

Date: 2026-10-10. Target claim: "no product/research artifact executes a troubleshooting
decision tree retrieved/generated from a real equipment manual, with per-step citations +
refusal on missing evidence, evaluated on a public benchmark."

Method: ~12 query angles across site:devpost.com, site:lablab.ai, GitHub, arXiv, vendor
sites (Yonyx/DeciZone/Zingtree/Gixo), aviation-maintenance vendors; direct page/repo/paper
reads for every candidate counterexample.

## VERDICT: PARTIALLY_DIFFERENTIATED

The full five-part conjunction (execution ∧ equipment-manual-grounding ∧ per-node citations ∧
refusal ∧ public benchmark) SURVIVES — no counterexample found combining all five. But the
"we EXECUTE decision trees, not just retrieve them" headline alone is BROKEN: a Microsoft
research framework published at FSE 2026 executes LLM-extracted troubleshooting DAGs, Amazon
runs a production SOP-tree executor, and a 2025 Sensors paper publishes interactive equipment
fault-tree execution. The claim survives only when pitched as the full conjunction; it must
never be pitched as "execution" or "interactive guided troubleshooting" alone.

## Implementation feasibility in 4–5 effective build days (2 people)

- Day 1–1.5: manual ingestion (Docling/MinerU with page provenance) + LLM extraction of
  troubleshooting sections into a JSON DAG with node-level source spans; validate on 2–3
  copyright-usable manuals (Industrial-Instruction CC-BY-NC Panasonic subset, or manuals
  cleared for the event). Text extraction is the simple case — do NOT attempt flowchart
  image extraction (VLM relation extraction shown broken, rel F1 < 0.11).
- Day 2: stateful execution engine + question-at-a-time UI (CLI/web), answer→branch,
  per-node citation rendering (page + revision).
- Day 2.5: refusal rule — branch without manual evidence → refuse, log, offer fallback
  retrieval, never free-guess.
- Day 3–4: eval harness — ~60–100 scenarios derived from Industrial-Instruction QA pairs;
  metrics: path accuracy, citation precision, refusal correctness (FaulT-Bench-style
  false-premise cases as a stress set). This is the long pole — start authoring Day 1.
- Day 4.5–5: AMD integration (extraction + inference LLM on ROCm — vLLM/llama.cpp ROCm on
  AMD GPU = the meaningful AMD part) + demo polish.
  Verdict: MEDIUM-HIGH risk on eval-scenario authoring, otherwise feasible. Manual
  licensing is the external risk — real OEM manuals are gatekept; the demo must run on
  corpus manuals with clear rights.

## Pitch-sound risk

What the pitch sounds like to a knowledgeable judge: **"StepFly for equipment manuals"**
(or: "Amazon's SOP-tree agent with citations"). Mitigations:
1. Never lead with "we execute decision trees" — lead with "execution with EVIDENCE
   ACCOUNTABILITY: every node cites the manual page/revision it came from, and the tree
   refuses to continue where the manual has no answer."
2. Cite StepFly, the Amazon agent, and the IETM paper as related work in the deck itself —
   preempt the judge who knows them; position them as cloud-ops/SOP cousins, us as the
   equipment-documentation slice they don't touch.
3. The public-benchmark evaluation (Industrial-Instruction) is the single most defensible
   originality element — no counterexample has one; make it a headline result, not a footnote.
4. Differentiate from FieldMate (same-event-risk lablab project): theirs guides through
   pre-approved procedures; ours extracts and cites from real manuals and refuses.
